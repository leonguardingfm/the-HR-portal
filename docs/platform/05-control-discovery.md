# 05 — Control discovery: the question list

Rostering is the part of this platform that looks simple in a specification and is not. It is where
Control's day happens, and the software has to fit how they **actually** work rather than how a
process document says they do. So this session is not a demo and not a sign-off; it is finding out
what breaks.

**Who should be in it:** the people who build the rota and the people who work the phones at 2am —
not only whoever manages them. The night shift knows things the day shift does not.

**How long:** two hours, or two sessions of an hour. Question block 1 alone is worth an hour.

**What to bring:** last week's actual rota, ideally a week that went wrong. And the WhatsApp
history from a night when cover fell through.

**Ground rule worth saying out loud at the start:** every "we just ring round" and "we usually
know" is exactly what we are looking for. Undocumented knowledge is not a confession, it is the
requirement.

> **The one thing this session must not produce:** agreement that the plan is fine. If nobody
> describes something that would break it, the questions were too comfortable.

---

## 1. How the rota actually gets built

The most important block. Everything downstream depends on the answers.

1. **Walk me through building next week's rota, from the blank page.** Where do you start — the
   sites, the officers, or the gaps left from last week?
2. **What do you build it in, and what is open beside it?** The spreadsheet, INDEL, WhatsApp, a
   notebook, a whiteboard, someone's memory.
3. **How far ahead is the rota published?** And how far ahead is it *reliable*?
4. **How much of next week is a copy of this week?** If it is mostly a copy, the system should
   start from the copy. If it is not, we have the model wrong.
5. **Which posts are fixed to one officer, and which are a pool?** Does the client care who turns
   up, or only that someone competent does?
6. **How do you know an officer is available?** Do they tell you, do you ask, or do you assume until
   told otherwise?
7. **Who is allowed to change a published rota?** And who finds out when it changes?
8. **What makes a rota "finished"?** Is there a moment it is issued, or does it just stop changing?

## 2. Cover, and what happens when it fails

This is where the money and the client relationships are.

9. **An officer calls in sick at 5pm for a 7pm shift. Walk me through the next twenty minutes.**
   Exactly who you ring, in what order, and how you decide.
10. **How do you pick who to ring?** Nearest, cheapest, most willing, least likely to say no,
    whoever answers?
11. **What do you do when nobody answers?** Is there a point at which you tell the client, and who
    makes that call?
12. **How often does a shift go completely uncovered?** Once a week, once a month, never?
13. **Does anyone routinely work more hours than they should?** How would you know before it
    happened, and does anything stop it?
14. **Are there officers you would never put on certain sites?** Why — client request, competence,
    a history? Where is that written down at the moment?
15. **How do last-minute changes reach the officer?** And how do you know they saw it?

## 3. Book-on, and the reality of it

16. **Today, how do you know an officer arrived?** They ring you, you ring them, or you find out
    when they do not?
17. **How often is someone late but fine?** Fifteen minutes late with traffic is not a no-show, and
    the system should not treat it as one. What is the real tolerance?
18. **Which posts have a site phone, and which do not?** The site phone is the strongest routine
    record we can get, and it changes the design where it exists.
19. **What happens at handover?** Does the outgoing officer wait for the incoming one, and is there
    a window where both are on post?
20. **Are there sites with no mobile signal at the post?** This is the question that decides how
    much has to work offline.
21. **Would officers accept a book-on on their own phone?** Honestly — including the ones who would
    make it a problem. And is there anyone without a phone that could do it?

## 4. Check calls and welfare

The process is confirmed. These are the edges it does not cover.

22. **Who makes the hourly check call — the officer rings in, or Control rings out?** They are
    different systems.
23. **Which sites is it actually hourly on?** Does any client contract specify something different?
24. **When a call is missed, what do you try, in what order?** We have it as: mobile, then site
    phone, then other officers, then the client's contact. Is that right, and is anything missing?
25. **How long does the whole ladder take in practice?** From missed call to someone setting off for
    site — five minutes or forty?
26. **Who actually goes to site?** Is there someone on call for it every night, or is it whoever can
    be reached?
27. **Should a lone-working post escalate faster?** We have deliberately not assumed it does.
28. **Has this ever ended badly?** Any near miss is worth more than the whole rest of this session.
29. **Do you log the attempts you make?** We need each attempt logged, because it is what advances
    the escalation. Is that realistic at 3am, and what would make it easier?

## 5. The Control–HR handover

Where the delays are, and the reason the platform timestamps it.

30. **When a client asks for a new officer, what do you do first?** How do you check whether the
    existing pool can cover it?
31. **How long does that pool check usually take?** And how often does it succeed?
32. **How do you hand a requirement to HR?** What does HR get, and what do they have to ask for?
33. **How do you find out a new officer is ready to be rostered?** And how long after they were
    actually ready?
34. **Who allocates the PIN, and when?** Where does the client's own reference (PRN) come from?

## 6. The two Control teams

35. **What is the split between Alpha and Bravo in practice?** We have it as workload and
    communication, not client or geography. Still true?
36. **What happens at your handover between teams?** What gets lost?
37. **Does an officer belong to one team?** Can they move, and who moves them?
38. **What is in the WhatsApp groups that is nowhere else?** This is a real question, not a
    criticism — that content is a requirement we have not captured.

## 7. What you would not give up

39. **What does the current way of working do well?** Something works, or the operation would have
    stopped. What is it, and what would we break by replacing it?
40. **What is the single worst part of the job that software could fix?** Answered without
    reference to anything we have shown you.
41. **What would make you stop using a new system in week one?** Slow, too many clicks, needing a
    laptop at 2am, not working on your phone?
42. **If the system were down on a Friday night, what would you do?** There has to be an answer, and
    it has to be one that works. It is decision **E2**.
43. **What have I not asked about that matters?** Leave this open, and leave time for it.

---

## Afterwards

Three things to write up while it is fresh:

1. **Anything that contradicts the plan.** Those go into the decision log as new E-series entries,
   not into the "nice to have" pile.
2. **Every number they gave** — tolerances, how far ahead, how long the ladder takes, how often
   cover fails. Those become the configurable defaults, and a real number beats a chosen one.
3. **Whatever is currently held in someone's head.** Which officers suit which sites, who will
   answer the phone at midnight, which clients tolerate a late start. That knowledge is the most
   valuable thing in the operation and the most fragile, and getting it out of people's heads and
   into data is a large part of what this platform is for.

Then the rostering estimate can be made. Not before.
