# 01 — Process and Compliance Review

## 1. What BS 7858:2019 actually asks for

The standard is a code of practice for screening people who work in a secure environment. Its
objective is narrow and worth stating plainly: **gather enough verified background information
to make an informed, risk-based decision about employing someone.** It is explicitly *not* a
recruitment process. It sits alongside recruitment and selection, and it deliberately does not
cover the application process, right to work, or job competency [Clause 1].

Two things in the 2019 edition matter for us:

- **Character references were removed.** They were considered too easy to abuse. We should not
  build them.
- **Automation is recognised and permitted** — but where any part of screening is automated, the
  provisions of the standard still apply [7.1]. So the portal can chase, calculate and pre-fill
  freely, as long as the evidence trail and the human sign-offs remain intact.

Social media and open-source internet checks are mentioned but are **not** a provision of the
standard. They may only be used as additional information, with care taken not to introduce
unlawful discrimination. Recommendation: leave them out of the portal for now.

### 1.1 The four gates

The standard defines a sequence of gates. The portal's whole job on the vetting side is to make
these gates visible and hard to skip.

| Gate | Clause | What must be true | What it unlocks |
|------|--------|-------------------|-----------------|
| **Information complete** | 7.3.2 | Full personal details, 5-year address history, full education/employment/self-employment/unemployment/gap history across the screening period, convictions and cautions including motoring and pending actions, bankruptcy/CCJ/IVA declarations, signed authorisation, signed declaration | Checks may begin |
| **Preliminary checks** | 7.4 | Screening file opened; all requested information supplied and reviewed to confirm the person is *likely* to complete screening; identity confirmed from original documents; current address confirmed; global watchlist/sanctions check; public record search via a credit reference agency | Limited screening may proceed |
| **Limited screening** | 7.5.2 | Continuous career and history confirmed for **at least the 3 years** immediately before the application date (or back to age 16); administrator named; **controller has reviewed and confirmed the file** | An offer of **conditional employment** may be made [7.5.1] |
| **Full screening** | 7.7 | The **whole screening period** verified with **no unverified gap longer than 31 days**; at least one of SIA licence / NPCC Appendix C / DBS-equivalent disclosure held or obtained [7.7j] | An offer of **confirmed employment** may be made |

An offer of conditional employment additionally requires that **the level of risk in the intended
role has been evaluated, deemed acceptable, and documented** [7.5.1a]. That is a recordable
decision, not a formality, and the portal should capture it.

### 1.2 The screening period and the clock

- The **screening period** is not less than **5 years** before employment starts, or back to age
  16 if that is more recent [3.13]. It must be extended for contractual, legislative or
  industry-standard reasons [7.3.2b].
- **Full screening must complete within 12 weeks** of conditional employment starting for 5-year
  screening, or **16 weeks** for 10-year screening [7.6].
- A single extension of **up to 4 further weeks** is possible, but only with evidence that written
  verification requests were made *and* approval by top management or an authorised person, with
  the decision recorded and easily retrievable [7.6].
- If screening has not completed successfully, **the person should not continue in relevant
  employment** [7.6]. Conditional employment ends [7.5.1].
- Records must show **prominently** the date conditional employment commenced and the date it is
  to cease if screening does not complete [7.2].

This is the deadline engine for the whole portal. Every conditionally employed officer is on a
countdown, and management needs to see that countdown.

### 1.3 Exceptions that need top management

Three situations require a named senior person to review the evidence and **sign to accept the
risk** [7.4f, Form 5]:

1. Single or multiple CCJs totalling more than **£10,000**, whether satisfied or not.
2. The individual is bankrupt.
3. The individual is, or was, a director of another organisation.

Where a concern surfaces in the public record search, the individual must first be **invited to
make representation** about it before any decision [7.4f].

Separately, a **statutory declaration** may substitute for verification only where the process
genuinely cannot be completed, and only for **one unverified period of no more than six months**
within the most recent five years — with prior documented top management approval [7.7i].

### 1.4 The people doing the screening

- The **screening controller** is responsible for making sure screening is carried out correctly.
  The **screening administrator** does the work [3.10, 3.11]. These are two distinct roles.
- Both must themselves be screened to BS 7858, and **no one may screen themselves** [6.1].
- Both must sign a confidentiality/non-disclosure agreement covering employment and
  post-employment [6.1].
- Where interviewing, screening, and the decision to employ or terminate sit in different
  departments, those departments must be co-ordinated with **particular attention to the division
  of functions and authority for internal control purposes** [6.1].
- Training must cover the standard, data protection law, and regulatory requirements; it must be
  **reviewed at least annually**; and training records must be accurately maintained [6.2].

The portal should therefore hold a small **vetting team competence register** — who is a
controller, who is an administrator, their own screening status, NDA on file, training dates and
next review date.

### 1.5 Records and retention

| Situation | Retention | Clause |
|-----------|-----------|--------|
| Unsuccessful at preliminary screening | Minimum **12 months**, then disposed of securely | 11.1 |
| During employment | Screening file retained | 11.2 |
| After employment ends | **7 years** for: written information provided, signed statement, proof of identity, continuous career/history confirmation, verification progress sheet, employment verification, statutory declaration, acceptance of risk | 11.3 |

A **separate file per individual**, with conditionally-employed files **identified separately**
from other employee files, plus a **verification progress sheet per individual** [7.2].

Note the data-minimisation point in the standard's example declaration: criminal background
information is **not retained** — only the fact and outcome of the check. The portal should
follow that.

### 1.6 Verification integrity rules

These are small but they are exactly the sort of thing an auditor tests, and exactly the sort of
thing a portal can enforce for free:

- When verifying by telephone, the number called must be **established independently**. A number
  supplied by the candidate must not be relied upon [7.5.2a].
- Where a reference arrives by text or email, the sender's contact details must be verified by
  direct contact, the source and integrity checked, and **records of how that was achieved
  maintained** [7.7].
- A **current** employer must not be contacted without the individual's prior written permission
  [7.7b]. If permission is withheld until an offer is made, the individual must be told the offer
  can be withdrawn if screening is not concluded satisfactorily, and the current employment period
  must initially be verified from documents [7.3.3a].
- Identity documents: photographic wherever possible, **visual inspection of originals**, a copy
  retained, and a record of **who examined and copied** the original. Compare the document against
  the person in front of you and check for tampering [7.4c].
- If the person holds a current SIA licence: see the original **and** verify it against the public
  register, retaining **a copy of the register search result** [7.4c1].
- Public record search via a credit reference agency must cover electoral roll presence, linked
  addresses for the previous five years, CCJs including IVAs, bankruptcy orders, aliases and date
  of birth. It is **not a credit check** [7.4f].

### 1.7 Beyond direct hires

- **Subcontractors and agency staff** doing relevant work on our behalf must either provide
  evidence they operate to BS 7858 (UKAS-accredited certification covering BS 7858, or SIA
  Approved Contractor status, **plus a written statement that the specific individuals supplied
  were screened**) or be fully screened by us [Clause 8].
- **Ancillary staff** — administration, maintenance, cleaning — must be screened if they have
  access to sensitive information, assets or equipment, and we must have procedures that stop
  unscreened people getting that access [Clause 9].
- **Existing officers** already in relevant employment must be screened to the standard. People
  already screened under earlier editions need not be re-screened *provided evidence of the
  previous screening can be clearly demonstrated* [7.1].
- If screening is **outsourced**, we retain ultimate responsibility and must **review the
  completed screening file** ourselves [6.3].

## 3. Gaps and inconsistencies to resolve

Raised for clarification rather than assumed. **Five have now been answered (Sept 2026)** and are
marked RESOLVED below; the rest still need a decision. The two items originally flagged as
material — 3.1 and 3.2 — both came back clean: the process is compliant in practice, and the
written description was misleading about its own order rather than wrong.

### 3.1 Order of screening and deployment — RESOLVED

This was raised as the material concern, because the written process put the "online history
checks" in step 6 alongside the Casper hire, after the Employment Contract had been signed in
step 5.

**Confirmed (Sept 2026):** officers are deployed to a client site **only after initial screening**.
The one element that runs after deployment is **verification of the five-year career history**,
which completes within the 12 weeks BS 7858 allows. Everything else is complete before anyone goes
to site.

That is compliant, and on one point it is **stricter than the standard's minimum**: the standard
treats the criminality element [7.7j] as part of full screening, whereas we complete it before
deployment. Worth keeping, and worth recording as a deliberate local policy rather than letting it
drift.

So the written process was misleading about its own order rather than wrong in practice. The
portal will enforce the confirmed order:

```
Application complete  ->  Interview  ->  Preliminary checks (7.4)
      ->  Limited screening: 3-year history (7.5.2)
      ->  Identity, address, sanctions, public record, criminality, right to work
      ->  Controller review + documented risk evaluation (7.5.1)
      ->  CONDITIONAL offer: Welcome Pack, contract signed        [12-WEEK CLOCK STARTS]
      ->  Onboarding admin: PIN, Casper hire, Watch List, Maps, Recruitment Sheet
      ->  DEPLOYABLE
      ->  Five-year career history verified within 12 weeks (7.7)
      ->  Controller review  ->  CONFIRMED EMPLOYMENT
```

The post-onboarding admin tasks stay exactly where they are. The only thing that moves is the
*recording* of checks that already happen.

**Two consequences worth noting.** First, the 12-week clock now covers a single, well-defined
piece of work — five-year history verification — which makes it far easier to forecast and to
staff. Second, because the Employment Contract is signed before that work finishes, the contract
must state that confirmed employment depends on satisfactory completion of full screening within
the period allowed, and that conditional employment ends if it does not [7.5.2]. That wording is
still to be checked — see C10 in [document 07](07-open-questions.md).

### 3.2 Consent to screen — RESOLVED

The concern was that written authorisation must exist before we approach employers, government
departments or a credit reference agency [7.3.2f, 7.3.3], and that the Declaration & Consent
appeared to arrive only with the Welcome Pack.

**Confirmed (Sept 2026):** the Casper application form already collects everything clause 7.3.2
requires, which includes the written authorisation [7.3.2f] and the signed declaration [7.3.2g].
The Welcome Pack is additionally returned signed the same day or the next. Consent therefore
exists before checks begin, and nothing needs to move.

The standard explicitly permits this arrangement — its example Form 3 "can be used as a separate
form or as part of the application form" [7.3.2, Note 6].

One detail the portal should still capture per employer rather than once per candidate: the
**permission-to-contact flag**. A current employer must not be approached without the individual's
prior written permission [7.7b], and where that permission is withheld until an offer is made, the
individual has to be told the offer can be withdrawn if screening is not concluded satisfactorily
[7.3.3a]. A single blanket consent on the form does not carry that distinction.

### 3.3 The interview is not in the written process

The written process runs application → Welcome Pack, with no interview step, yet there is an
"Interview Sheet". BS 7858 is direct: **interview the individual before any offer of employment
is made** [7.3.4].

**To confirm:** is an interview held, who holds it, and is the outcome recorded anywhere we could
show an auditor? The portal should record interviewer, date, outcome and notes as a gate.

### 3.4 "Online history checks" needs decomposing

This is one line in the current process but four distinct BS 7858 requirements:

1. Identity verification against original documents, with an SIA public register check where a
   licence is held [7.4c]
2. Current address confirmation [7.4d]
3. Global watchlist / sanctions screening, including the HM Treasury consolidated list [7.4e]
4. Public record search via a credit reference agency — electoral roll, linked addresses,
   CCJs/IVAs, bankruptcy, aliases, DOB [7.4f]

Plus, separately, employment and education verification across the screening period [7.7], and
the criminality element [7.7j].

**To confirm:** which of these do we do today, and by what means? The portal should track each as
its own check with its own evidence, status and owner.

### 3.5 Screening period — RESOLVED

**Confirmed (Sept 2026): we screen to five years.** The clock is therefore **12 weeks** for every
file [7.6], and there is one number to explain rather than two.

The portal keeps the 10-year path available but unused, for two reasons the standard raises
directly: the screening period has to be extended for contractual or legislative reasons or
specific industry standards [7.3.2b], and some insurers require BS 7858 as a policy condition
**and impose additional requirements such as a longer screening period** [Clause 1, Note 2]. So it
stays a per-client setting with a five-year default, rather than being hard-coded.

Still worth confirming: whether our insurers impose anything beyond the standard — C8 in
[document 07](07-open-questions.md).

### 3.6 Casper application form — RESOLVED

If it does not, no amount of portal chasing will fix it — we will just be chasing a fixed form
forever. The form needs to capture at minimum:

- Full name, plus all previous names and aliases used during the screening period
- Full address history with from/to dates covering 5 years
- Date of birth, National Insurance number
- Right to work evidence
- SIA licence number and expiry, and all licences held including expired ones `[SV]`
- Continuous education, employment, self-employment, unemployment and gap history across the
  whole screening period, with from/to dates and employer contact details
- Explicit **permission-to-contact** flag per employer, so current employers are not approached
  without written permission [7.7b]
- All cautions and convictions including motoring offences and pending actions
- All bankruptcy proceedings and court judgments including satisfied ones, and IVAs
- The misrepresentation acknowledgement, the written authorisation, and the signed declaration
  [7.3.2e, f, g]

**Confirmed (Sept 2026):** the Casper form contains everything clause 7.3.2 requires, **and it can
be changed** if we find a gap. That removes the worst-case scenario, which was chasing candidates
forever against a form we could not fix.

Two things to verify against the live form rather than assume, because they are the items most
often missing in practice: the **permission-to-contact flag held per employer** rather than as one
blanket consent, and **all SIA licences held including expired ones** `[SV]`. Both are cheap to add
and both save a re-request later.

### 3.7 In-house or outsourced vetting — RESOLVED

**Confirmed (Sept 2026): vetting is done in-house.** The outsourcing provisions of clause 6.3 do
not apply, so there is no "provider file received and reviewed" step to build.

The provider guide supplied (Staffvetting.com) is therefore useful as a **document-acceptance
standard** rather than as a description of who does the work. Its rules are stricter than the
standard's wording and they are the reason documents get rejected, so the portal encodes them as
upload-time validation — see §3.9 below.

Doing it in-house makes clauses 6.1 and 6.2 our responsibility directly: the screening controller
and administrators must themselves be screened to BS 7858, must not screen themselves, must sign
confidentiality agreements covering employment and post-employment, and must have training records
maintained and reviewed at least annually. With a two-person vetting team this needs a specific
arrangement — see §4 below.

### 3.8 The spreadsheets overlap

There are at least four: Sourcing Sheet, Interview Sheet, 2025 Recruitment Sheet, and Watch List
— plus Maps, Casper and Indeed. The same person's name is typed into most of them. Every one of
those re-entries is a chance to create a duplicate, a spelling variant, or a record that drifts
out of date.

The portal's core answer is in document 02: **one candidate record**, created once, that carries
the person from sourcing to confirmed employment, with the sheets becoming views of it rather than
separate files.

### 3.9 Evidence rules from the provider guide `[SV]`

These are the provider's house rules, stricter than the standard's wording, and worth encoding as
validation because they are the reason documents get rejected and re-requested:

- **At least two different documents per career-history period** — one dated at the start, one at
  the end — and they **must be different document types** (not two payslips; a payslip and a P60).
- Name **and** date must be visible on every document; for third-party documents, the issuing
  organisation's name and date must be visible too.
- Address document age limits: bank / credit card / utility (not mobile) / benefit statements
  under **3 months**; council tax, HMRC documents, P45/P60, mortgage statements under **12 months**.
- Gaps over **31 days** need documentary evidence, with specific acceptable evidence per gap type
  (maternity, military, redundancy, travel, volunteering, illness, caring, unemployed with or
  without benefits).
- Bank statements may be redacted down to name, date, address and the relevant payment line.

Encoding these as form-level validation at upload time is one of the highest-value automations
available to us — it stops the request-reject-rerequest loop before it starts.

### 3.10 Smaller items to confirm

- **Ancillary staff** (office and admin) — are they screened? Clause 9 says they should be if they
  have access to sensitive information, assets or equipment.
- **Subcontractors / agency cover** — do we use any? Clause 8 applies if so.
- **Existing officer pool** — can we demonstrate BS 7858 screening for everyone currently
  deployed? If not, retrospective screening is required [7.1, Clause 10].
- **Retention** — is anything currently disposed of at 12 months (unsuccessful) or 7 years
  (leavers)? Is there a secure disposal step?
- **WhatsApp sourcing** — how are those enquiries retained as part of the recruitment record, and
  what is our data protection position on holding them?
- **Right to work** — is there a follow-up date process for time-limited leave? This is outside
  BS 7858's scope but is a separate legal obligation and belongs in the portal as its own check.
- **Vulnerable groups** — do any posts bring officers into contact with children or vulnerable
  adults? That may need a higher level of disclosure [7.7j, Note 6].

A consolidated list of everything needing an answer is in [document 07](07-open-questions.md).

That already gives the separation of functions clause 6.1 asks for: recruiting and screening sit
with different people, so nobody is both interviewing a candidate and signing off their screening
file.

### 4.1 Controller and administrator with a two-person team

BS 7858 needs two distinct roles on every file: the **screening administrator** does the work, the
**screening controller** is responsible for it being done correctly and reviews the file [3.10,
3.11, 7.5.2b]. The reviewing controller cannot be the person who built the file.

With Anas and Talha this works cleanly by **alternating per file**: Anas administers, Talha
reviews; Talha administers, Anas reviews. Both need to be trained and recorded as competent in
both roles, and the portal enforces the pairing so the same person can never appear twice on one
file. This is what the demonstration data shows.

### 4.2 The gap: screening the screeners

Both Anas and Talha must themselves be screened to BS 7858, and neither may screen themselves
[6.1]. For Anas's own file, Talha can be the administrator — but then the controller has to be
someone who is neither the subject nor the administrator, which rules out both of them.

**This needs a decision.** Two workable options:

1. **Farhan reviews those two files** as controller. He is already the authorised person for risk
   acceptance, so he is a natural fit — but he would need to be trained to clause 6.2 and screened
   himself, and he cannot be the controller on his own file either.
2. **Buy those specific files in** from an accredited provider, and review the returned file
   ourselves as clause 6.3 requires. Two files is a small, one-off cost.

Whichever we choose, it also has to answer who screens **Farhan**, and who holds the training and
NDA records for all three.

### 4.3 What the portal holds for this

A small **vetting team competence register** in Admin: who is a controller, who is an
administrator, each person's own screening status and file reference, NDA on file, training dates
and next annual review date [6.1, 6.2]. The rule the system enforces is that a role grant lapses
when the annual training review does, rather than quietly continuing.
